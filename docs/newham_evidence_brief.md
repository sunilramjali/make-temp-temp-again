# Newham evidence brief: who is in temporary accommodation, what is offered, is social housing enough

Final draft, 7 November 2026. Findings are **descriptive**: they show patterns and do not say what causes them. Every figure comes from a published source; links, and how far each was checked, are in [`source_links.md`](source_links.md). The same figures are in the dashboard (`app/`).

**Scope:** temporary accommodation (TA) in Newham, compared with London and England. The project's focus group is households with dependent children and pregnant women.

---

## 1. Headlines

1. **Scale.** On 31 March 2026 Newham had **7,561 households in TA**, the most of any London borough: **6.0% of all households**, about 1 in 17 (London 2.1%, England 0.6%). Of these, 5,453 were households with children (10,952 children).
2. **Who.** Newham's TA population is more family-heavy than London's (72% of households have dependent children vs 67%), and its families are less often single-adult (47% vs 56%). Among households whose ethnicity is recorded, 41% of main applicants are Asian (23% Bangladeshi), 33% Black and 17% White. But **ethnicity is not recorded for 30%** of Newham's TA households, so treat these shares with care.
3. **Where and how long.** 63% of Newham's families in TA are in nightly-paid, privately managed self-contained accommodation (London 49%). 72% of those families have been there a year or more, and 843 families for five years or more.
4. **Social housing.** 41,223 households were on Newham's housing register at 31 March 2025 (highest in London, up 58% since 2018). Newham made **711 new social lettings in 2024/25**, down from 966 the year before. Social-housing churn is 1.4% of stock a year, the lowest of the boroughs that submitted full CORE data (Camden and Greenwich did not submit this year, so their rates are unreliable).
5. **Current solution.** Newham's stated approach is to move families into private rented homes, increasingly outside the borough. Its 2025 placements policy says it needs to procure accommodation outside London to keep it affordable. Uptake evidence exists for only 3 of 11 recorded services (counts of duties ended, lettings and Homefinder moves), and **no published evidence of outcomes** was found for any. That is a gap, not a finding that they don't work. In January to March 2026, more Newham duties ended through private rented offers (36) than social housing offers (25), out of 82; one quarter, so possibly not typical (section 8).

### Out of area (TA1, note 36)

3,173 of Newham's 7,561 households (42.0%) are recorded as out of area (London: 35,460, 46.6%). Note 36 says this can include households owed a duty but with no accommodation secured, so it may overstate placements slightly. Newham's own 2021 strategy gave 63% of placements in Newham, 34% in another London borough and 3% outside London. That is a different measure and date, so don't treat the two as a trend.

## 4. Current solutions: what Newham's policies offer

This section records what is **offered** in published policy. Evidence of **uptake or outcomes** is kept separate (end of section), as the project rules require.

### Documents read (saved in `policy/newham/`)

1. **Housing Allocation Scheme v3.0** (council file dated 4 Sept 2025), 20 pages.
2. **Housing placements policy: location of accommodation offers for homeless households**, 7 pages (no version date printed).
3. **Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy 2021-2026**, 40 pages (key sections read).
4. **Cabinet decision 23 Oct 2025**: read only through a third-party summary (opencouncil.network). The cabinet report and its appendices were not opened.

### What the placements policy says (document 2)

- **Aim:** accommodation within Newham wherever possible, "subject to the supply of suitable and affordable accommodation" (p.2).
- **Zones** (p.3-4): Zone A is in Newham or within 30 minutes' public transport of its boundary. Zone B is outside Newham within 90 minutes. Zone C is more than 90 minutes. Households go to A if suitable homes exist, then B, then C.
- **Who gets priority for Zone A** (p.3): households with a child who has an EHC plan in Newham; a child subject to a Child Protection Plan, or families helped by Children and Young People Services where a move would have a significant negative welfare impact; severe, enduring health conditions treatable only in Newham; significant care packages that can't transfer; long-standing caring arrangements for a family member in Newham. Further criteria on p.3-4 cover children in exam years (Years 10-13), work (six months' continuous employment of 16+ hours a week) and risk of gender-based or gang-related violence. **The text extraction does not show which zone each of those last three belongs to. Check the PDF layout before citing them.**
- **Affordability:** it aims for rents within Local Housing Allowance, and says: "Given the context of rapidly rising rents in Newham and the rest of London, we need to procure accommodation out of London so that we can continue to provide affordable accommodation" (p.2).
- **One offer:** "We will usually only make one offer of accommodation to a household", to be accepted the day it is made (p.2).
- **Ending the duty:** an offer "that ends the duty (which will usually be in the private rented sector)" (p.2).
- **Support for out-of-borough moves** (p.6): information on the new area, schools and childcare, benefits and local services, help contacting social care, and guaranteeing rent to private landlords for up to a year where a resident can't evidence income.
- **Pregnant women:** not mentioned in this policy.
- **Review:** monitored and reviewed annually (p.6). The annual review outputs were not found.

### What the 2021-26 strategy commits to (document 3, section references)

- **5.1 Reduce nightly-booked accommodation**, described as expensive and unstable, sometimes forcing several moves.
- **5.4 Improved move-on offer to TA residents:** dedicated resettlement support officers; support to use Homefinder (a service finding secure tenancies across the UK; "over 30 households from Newham have used" it at the time of writing); support to seek stable homes outside social housing, including private renting and shared ownership.
- **2.4.2 Tenancy sustainment hub:** new Tenancy Sustainment Officers to help people when they first move into private renting.
- **Expectations:** officers are to tell applicants that the housing register wait is "the longest of any London Borough, with many households waiting over 10 years", and that TA "is anything but temporary" (PDF p.21).
- **Context it gives (PDF p.13):** TA at 48 per 1,000 households in Newham against 17 in London, nightly-paid just under 60% of TA, and TA up more than 120% between 2011 and 2018. The passage doesn't state the date of the 48 and 17 figures. Today's file gives 59.8 and 21.1.

### Cabinet decision, 23 October 2025 (document 4, third-party summary, unverified)

Per the summary page: Cabinet approved a revised Housing Placement Policy; up to £1.7m extra for the Homelessness Response Programme; a "comprehensive review" of the Social Housing Allocations Policy; and an "extensive programme of review of all households in TA". Its stated objectives include "increased use of affordable, lawful out-of-borough options" and expanding access to the private rented sector. **TA household numbers and cost figures from this decision are not yet checked.** News reports quote £52.4m for a 2025/26 TA budget gap; that figure was not found in a council document.

### Evidence of uptake and outcomes

None found for: private rented sector offers, the rent guarantee, Homefinder moves after 2021, resettlement or tenancy sustainment officers, or the annual placement policy review. Nothing has been recorded in `policy/services_map.csv`, so "offered" and "uptake/outcome" cannot yet be compared.

## 5. Is social housing enough?

Short answer from the figures: **supply is small compared with demand on every measure available, and getting smaller.** The measures use different definitions and dates, so they show scale, not exact ratios.

### Waiting list (MHCLG Live Table 600, households on the register at 31 March; the 2018 figure is at 1 April 2018 per table note 3)

| Year | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Newham | 26,139 | 27,635 | 28,020 | 31,887 | 33,246 | 35,625 | 38,417 | **41,223** |

- Up 58% from 2018 to 2025. Highest of the 33 London boroughs in 2025 (next: Brent 36,534, Lambeth 29,187, Tower Hamlets 28,469). Newham holds 12.1% of the 341,009 households on London borough registers.
- **Caution (table note 1):** a register is not the same as households waiting. Councils review registers at different times and some households are on several. Brent's jump from 3,051 (2020) to 24,002 (2021) and Lambeth's fall from 38,131 to 29,187 (2025) look like changes in register practice, so cross-borough rankings need checking.

### New lettings (CORE, Table 1d)

| | 2022/23 | 2023/24 | 2024/25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Newham new social lettings (all types) | 930 | 966 | **711** |

- The 33 boroughs total 28,299 in the table, but **Barnet, Camden, Greenwich and Wandsworth did not submit CORE data in 2024/25** (table note 10), so that total and any ranking are incomplete. Newham did submit. Highest among the others: Lambeth 1,856, Hackney 1,662, Tower Hamlets 1,636. The figures are unweighted and as reported (note 9).
- Roughly 58 households on the register for each letting made in a year, and the TA caseload (7,561) is about 10.6 times annual lettings. These compare numbers from different dates and populations (many lettings go to people not in TA), so use them only to show scale.
- The council's "around 600 properties a year" in older consultation slides is not a current official figure. The CORE figure above replaces it.

### Churn and the rent gap (CORE, Table 1e)

- **448 social re-lets from 31,666 homes: 1.4% of stock** a year. Among boroughs that submitted CORE data in 2024/25, Newham has the lowest rate (next: Southwark 1.7%; highest: Richmond upon Thames 5.0%). Greenwich (1.2%) and Camden (1.4%) did not submit, so their rates are under-counts and can't be compared.
- Average weekly **social re-let rent £160.26** vs **market rent £390.23** (41.1%), per the table's rent source. The gap is about £230 a week, which is the context for families trying to leave TA by renting privately.

### Not available in the data used

- The share of Newham's lettings that went to homeless households or people in TA (in CORE's tenant tables, not opened).
- Waiting-list numbers by priority group.
- Table 602 (local authority dwellings let) holds England-level data only, not Newham.

## 6. Barriers to entry

From the 2025 Housing Allocation Scheme unless stated. Each is a rule on paper. Whether any of them actually blocks a TA family is not shown by these sources.

| Barrier | What the document says | Does it apply to a family owed a homelessness duty? |
|---|---|---|
| **Residency** | Must have lived in Newham continuously for 3 years (p.4) | No: exempt if owed a Part 7 homelessness duty by Newham (p.4) |
| **Placed here by another council** | Cannot register if placed in Newham by another authority under a homelessness duty (p.4) | Applies to households *other* councils place in Newham |
| **Moved away** | Cannot stay registered if they move out of borough after applying, unless an exception applies (p.4) | The Part 7 exemption would cover those placed out by Newham. Not stated for people who move for other reasons |
| **Immigration status** | People subject to immigration control who are restricted from applying cannot join (p.4) | Yes, if restricted |
| **Rent arrears / property debt** | Reduced bidding priority until debts cleared, unless a reasonable repayment plan exists (p.12-13). Only the emergency (Group A) group can be offered a home with arrears under 4 weeks' rent | Yes, if arrears exist. Private-sector arrears also count |
| **Behaviour** | Unacceptable or anti-social behaviour can exclude someone, including those accepted as homeless (p.4-5) | Yes |
| **Savings or assets** | £60,000 or more means reduced priority (p.4, 12) | Unlikely to affect most TA families |
| **Bidding limits** | Applicants may bid for up to two properties per cycle (p.10). Applicants who stop bidding on suitable homes can lose "enhanced bidding" status (p.10) | Applies to the choice-based route |
| **Refusing an offer** | Unreasonable refusal can end the council's duty to a household in TA (p.15). Most direct-offer groups get one offer (p.16) | Yes: the main consequence for TA families |
| **Bedroom entitlement** | Pregnant women get no bed space for the unborn child until after the birth (p.14). The p.14 table sets bedroom need by household makeup (e.g. a parent with two children of opposite sex, one over 10, needs a 3-bed). The scheme notes "high levels of overcrowding and high demand for larger family sized properties" (p.18) | Yes: relevant to the pregnant-household focus |
| **Accurate information** | False declarations can cost accrued waiting time or lead to exclusion (p.12-13) | Yes |

**Barriers on the private rented route** (strategy, section on LHA, PDF p.7): with 2019/20 Local Housing Allowance rates, under 2% of one-bedroom and under 5% of two-bedroom homes in the Outer East London rental area (which covers Newham) were affordable on LHA. These rates and the benefit cap have since changed, so this needs current data (Phase 6).

## 7. Limitations and further work

**Caveats**
- One quarter's snapshot, not a trend. Earlier MHCLG releases would give the time series.
- 30% of Newham's TA households have no recorded ethnicity.
- The policy documents are read from PDF text. Layout-dependent lists (placement zones) need a check against the PDF.
- The cabinet decision was seen only through a third-party summary.

**Further work**
1. Open the Oct 2025 Cabinet report and appendices (TA numbers, costs, the draft placement policy, equality impact assessment).
2. CORE tenant tables or dashboard for lettings to homeless households in Newham.
3. Earlier quarters of the MHCLG tables for a Newham trend.
4. Private rents and LHA (Phase 6) to put numbers on the affordability barrier.
5. GR's policy documents and the agreed `service_category` list, so these Newham policies can be entered in `services_map.csv` as "offered" rows, with uptake and outcome kept blank until evidence turns up.

## 8. Further evidence: exits, pregnancy, out-of-area placements, trend and supply

Sources: further sheets of the same MHCLG file (TA9, MD1 to MD3), Trust for London charts T87 and T88, and the GLA council homes statistics. All are in `data/sources.csv` and on the dashboard.

**London over time (T87, Trust for London from MHCLG).** London households in TA, average of four quarters: **48,678 in 2002, 74,860 in 2025**. Nightly-paid self-contained accommodation grew from 3,655 to 34,168 households and is now the largest type. There is a **series break at 2018/2019** (MHCLG changed collection), so compare within each side of the break.

**Boroughs in 2025 (T88, average of four quarters).** Newham had **59.1 households in TA per 1,000**, the highest in London (Westminster 37.3, Lambeth 32.3), on Trust for London's denominator. This is a different measure from MHCLG's 31 March 2026 snapshot (59.8), so the two are not a trend.

**How households leave TA (MD2, one quarter, Jan to Mar 2026).** Newham ended the main duty for 82 households: **36 (44%) by an accepted private rented offer, 25 (31%) by an accepted social housing offer**, and 11 (13%) left TA voluntarily. Across London, 62% of duty ends were social housing offers and 18% private rented; in England, 70% and 9%. This fits Newham's stated approach (section 4) of ending the duty mainly through private renting. It is one quarter, with small numbers, so it may not be typical.

**Pregnancy as a priority need (MD3, one quarter).** Of 180 Newham households newly owed the main duty, **10 (5.6%) had pregnancy recorded as their priority need** (London 2.7%, England 2.8%). This is a flow of new acceptances, not households in TA, and it undercounts: a pregnant woman in a family recorded under "dependent children" is not counted.

**Where out-of-area households are (TA9, 31 March 2026).** Of Newham's 3,173 out-of-area households, 2,758 were elsewhere in London, 219 in the East of England and 193 in the South East. 82% were in nightly-paid accommodation and 14% in private sector accommodation.

**New council homes (GLA programmes only).** Newham completed 1,457 and started 2,049 council homes through GLA programmes from September 2018 to March 2026. In 2025-26 it completed 379, second in London after Brent (549), out of 3,503 across London. Other council homes are reported by MHCLG, so this is part of new supply, not all of it.

**Services map.** `policy/services_map.csv` now has 11 Newham rows across the six service categories in the README, each citing its source page. Uptake is filled only where a document or dataset reports it (MD2 duty ends, Homefinder). No outcome evidence was found for any service.

**Not usable:** the LAHS 2025-26 file is the blank return form (no figures). It is kept for definitions only.
