# London temporary accommodation policy: summary and data needs

House London #3, 7 Nov 2026. Companion to `claude/newham-ta-policy-summary.md` and `claude/national-ta-policy-summary.md`. Problem: how to make temporary accommodation (TA) temporary again, by moving households into settled homes.

Source key:
- LP21 = The London Plan 2021 (GLA, March 2021), Chapter 4 Housing.
- DLP26 = Draft new London Plan (GLA, published for consultation 16 July 2026; consultation closed 15 Oct 2026). Read via trade press summaries only (Building, Lichfields, Pegasus, BusinessLDN). Not adopted.
- LHS18 = London Housing Strategy 2018, policies 7.1 and 7.2. Read only as quoted in Newham Homelessness Strategy 2021-26, p10.
- CHAP = GLA Council Homes Acquisition Programme pages and press releases (2023 to 2024).
- LC = London Councils, including the LSE London report "The temporary accommodation subsidy gap in London" (Oct 2025).
- TfL-data = Trust for London, temporary accommodation indicators (updated June and July 2026).
- NAT = MHCLG, Delivering a decade of renewal (July 2025). Uploaded copy.
- NHS21 = Newham Homelessness Strategy 2021-26. Uploaded copy.

## 1. Headline position (b)

- 74,860 London households were in TA in 2025, the highest since comparable data began in 2002; up 22% in two years. (TfL-data, TA over time)
- London had more than half (56%) of all households in TA in England. (Homelessness Impact, May 2026, citing government figures)
- 34,318 London households were placed in TA in a different local authority in 2025, almost six times the 2010 figure. (TfL-data)
- About 4,500 London households were in B&B-style TA in 2025, down more than 25% in a year. (Trust for London, latest data article)
- Every borough except Bexley and Hounslow has a higher share of households in TA than the England average. Newham is highest at 59.12 per 1,000 households, and highest for out-of-borough placements at 24.04 per 1,000 (2025). (TfL-data, by borough)
- Note: Newham's own strategy gave 48 per 1,000 (NHS21 p13, older data). Use the Trust for London figure for current comparisons and say which year.

## 2. London Plan: supply targets (a)

London Plan 2021 (LP21):
- Policy H1, Table 4.1: ten-year targets for net housing completions by borough, 2019/20 to 2028/29. London total 522,870, about 52,287 a year. (LP21 Table 4.1, via GLA and planning.data.gov.uk extract)
- Boroughs do not carry out their own housing need assessment but "must plan for, and seek to deliver, the housing targets in this Plan". (LP21 H1 supporting text)
- Strategic affordable housing target of 50% (confirmed as retained in DLP26 by Pegasus; check the exact LP21 wording in Policy H4).

Draft London Plan 2026 (DLP26), not adopted:
- Ten-year target of 558,000 homes (about 55,800 a year), with a longer-term aim of at least 850,000 homes by 2047. (BusinessLDN briefing; Pegasus)
- Government housing need for London given as 84,884 a year (revised down from 88,000 in the 2025 consultation). (BusinessLDN)
- About 10% of the ten-year target from Green Belt land; 50% strategic affordable target kept, with a new tiered threshold approach. (Pegasus)
- Newham's annual target falls by 654 homes compared with LP21. (Lichfields, 23 July 2026)

What the London Plan does not do (c): it sets targets for homes built, not for who lives in them. It has no target for TA, move-on, or lettings to homeless households, as far as the sources we read show.

## 3. Mayor and GLA policy on homelessness and TA (a)

London Housing Strategy 2018 (LHS18, policies 7.1 and 7.2, as summarised in NHS21 p10):
- Support councils to implement the Homelessness Reduction Act.
- About £8.5m a year for pan-London rough sleeping services.
- Work with councils on a "significantly more collaborative approach to securing private rented accommodation for homeless households".
- NHS21 notes "the powers of the GLA are limited in tackling homelessness". The statutory duty sits with boroughs.

Council Homes Acquisition Programme (CHAP):
- Launched 17 Nov 2023; funds councils to buy homes from the private market and convert them into social rented housing or TA.
- Ambition: councils to buy 10,000 homes over a decade. The first CHAP programme ran to March 2026.
- Follows Right to Buy-back, which brought over 1,500 homes into council ownership. (GLA press releases)
- A GLA-commissioned New Economics Foundation report claimed the 10,000 homes could cut boroughs' TA costs by £1.5bn over two decades. Treat as a projection.

GLA sub-programme for TA within the Affordable Homes Programme 2021-26, primarily to help councils acquire homes for TA. (GLA FOI, July 2024)

Social and Affordable Homes Programme 2026-2036: up to 30% of funding (up to £11.7bn) delivered by the GLA in London. (NAT Step 1)

## 4. Borough collaboration on TA (a)

- Capital Letters: London Councils company, MHCLG-supported, procuring TA and private rented homes for member boroughs. LSE evaluation reports 21 member boroughs, standardised landlord incentive payments (reducing inter-borough competition), a pan-London property standard, and a tenancy sustainment service.
- PLACE (Pan-London Accommodation Collaborative Enterprise): not-for-profit company set up in 2018, hosted by Tower Hamlets; GLA grant up to £11m for up to 200 modular homes for TA on meanwhile sites. (GLA DD2335)
- Inter-Borough Temporary Accommodation Agreement (IBTAA): pan-London agreement on rates and placements in other boroughs (dates back to 2011). A Trust for London inquiry reports its rates, like LHA, had not changed since 2020 despite rising rents.
- Setting the Standard: inter-borough inspection standard for single-person TA, especially HMO-style. (NHS21 4.5.2)

## 5. Roadblocks London sources name (b)

- Funding gap: London Councils (LSE London report) estimates a £740m TA shortfall across London boroughs, £202 per household; TA housing benefit subsidy frozen since 2011. Seven London boroughs rely on Exceptional Financial Support. (LSE news release)
- Spending equivalent to 11% of each household's council tax bill goes on TA. (LC)
- Landlords leaving: London Councils survey found a 120% increase in landlords leaving the TA sector (date of survey not confirmed).
- London Councils asks government to (1) align TA subsidy with current LHA, (2) raise LHA to reflect rents, (3) fund councils to build or buy TA. (LSE news release)
- Delivery gap: about 33,000 homes completed in 2023/24 against LP21's c.52,300 a year. (Lichfields, May 2025)

## 6. What we infer (c)

- London policy acts mainly on supply (London Plan targets, CHAP, SAHP share). There is no London-level target for getting households out of TA, matching the gap we found in the national plan.
- The GLA has limited homelessness powers; the tools that move people out of TA (allocations, private rented offers, LHA, subsidy) sit with boroughs and central government.
- Acquisitions (CHAP) can turn TA into settled homes directly, which is the closest London-level policy to "making TA temporary again".
- Newham's target falling in DLP26 while it has the highest TA rate in London is a possible tension worth flagging, but targets reflect land capacity, not need, so it is not evidence of failure on its own.

Smallest version for today: rank 33 boroughs by TA per 1,000 (TA1) and average length of stay (TA7), and set it against LP21 completions vs target. If high-TA boroughs are not the low-delivery boroughs, that supports the story that building alone is not getting people out of TA.
