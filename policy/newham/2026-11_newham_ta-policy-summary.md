# Newham temporary accommodation: policy summary and data needs

House London #3, 7 Nov 2026. Problem: how to make temporary accommodation (TA) temporary again, by moving households into settled homes.

Source key:
- HS = Newham Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Strategy 2021-2026
- FR = Newham Cabinet, Summer 2024 Finance Review Report (6 Aug 2024)
- WEB = Newham Council homelessness prevention and advice web pages (copy saved as docx, mentions July 2026)
- CRF = Newham Crisis and Resilience Fund Housing Payment Policy, April 2026 (v4.1) and Priority Categories
- NAT = MHCLG, Delivering a decade of renewal for social and affordable housing (July 2025)

Labels: (a) what policy says should happen, (b) what the data or documents report happened, (c) our inference.

## 1. Headline position

- (b) Newham's TA rate was the highest in London: 48 per 1,000 households vs 17 per 1,000 for London overall. TA households rose by more than 120% between 2011 and 2018. (HS, Homelessness in Newham, p13)
- (b) About 6,500 households in TA at end of 2023/24, up 14%. 6,685 homelessness applications in 2023/24, up 26%. (FR, 1.9)
- (b) The Council says "most households wait in temporary accommodation for several years" and there is "no set timeframe". (WEB, Accommodation offers)
- (b) Social housing: about 600 council and housing association homes become available a year (HS p11; WEB FAQ 2). Waiting list over 28,000 applicants (HS, 2021), over 38,000 households (FR, 2024), over 50,000 people as of July 2026 (WEB). Units differ, so do not chart these as one series.
- (b) Average wait for a 3-bed for a household in housing need: over 13 years. (HS p11)

## 2. What Newham's policy says it will do to move people on (a)

From HS Section 5, "Reduce the use of temporary accommodation":
- 5.1 Reduce nightly-booked accommodation. It was just under 60% of TA (HS p13). The foreword reports a 10% cut in households in nightly-paid TA.
- 5.2 Increase supply: Local Space partnership; buying properties for TA; council housebuilding (Affordable Homes for Newham) and the council company Populo; modular homes on small sites.
- 5.3 Work with private landlords; join Capital Letters and PLACE; a housing association lease giving 21 homes at LHA rents on two-year tenancies for households in TA.
- 5.4 Improved move-on offer: dedicated resettlement support officers; support to use HomefinderUK to move out of borough (over 30 households had done so).

From HS Section 4:
- 4.2 Resettlement advice officers and a working group for residents in TA.
- 4.7 Placements Policy prioritises in-borough placement for those with most need to stay.
- 4.8.2 Aim: a household moves only once into TA, then on to long-term housing.
- 4.9 Allocations policy changed in 2021, to be reviewed after one year for its effect on homeless households.

Current practice (WEB, Accommodation offers and PRS FAQ):
- The settled offer "is likely to be private rented accommodation" because social housing is very limited.
- One offer only. Refusing a suitable offer may end the main duty and the TA.
- Residents may find their own private rental and submit a "Property Identified by Resident" form; the Council checks it and can help with deposit or rent in advance.
- The Council cites the Renters' Rights Act 2025 (no no-fault evictions, controlled rent rises) as making private renting a more secure exit. Note: the web pages give both "from 1 May 2026" and "from April 2026" for the start date. Check before quoting.
- Moving to another borough generally removes the household from Newham's housing register. Accepting a private rental may also reduce register priority.

Money to help people move (CRF, April 2026):
- CRF Housing Payments replaced Discretionary Housing Payments.
- Priority 2: households in TA (s188/s193) affected by the benefit cap, usually 3-month awards tied to steps to become exempt from the cap.
- Lump sums for deposit or rent in advance, normally no more than one month's LHA (CRF 4.6, 4.7).
- Long-term awards above £150 a week unlikely (CRF 4.4). Benefit cap stated as £23,000 a year (CRF 2.7).

## 3. Roadblocks named in the documents (b)

- Not enough social homes: council stock down 35% (9,000 homes) in 25 years; 47% of Right to Buy homes rented out privately. (HS p11)
- LHA does not cover rents: in the Outer East London rental area, under 2% of 1-bed, under 5% of 2-bed and 4-bed, and under 10% of 3-bed homes were affordable on 2019/20 LHA, vs 8% of the market across London. (HS p7)
- Benefit cap limits larger families. (HS p7; CRF 2.7, 4.5)
- Private rented supply shrinking: 47% reduction since the pandemic; rents up 20.9% in Q1 2022 vs 2017-19. (FR 1.10)
- Low incomes: lowest average wages in London; 49% in poverty after housing costs. (HS p11)
- Insecure private renting feeds back into TA: Newham's eviction rate 3.2 per 1,000 households vs 1.7 for London. (HS p11)
- Funding: TA overspend £22m in 2023/24, £40m forecast for 2024/25; TA is 10% of the budget and could reach a third. (FR 2.2, 2.3, 2.5)
- Homelessness Prevention Grant 2023/24 worked out at £1,816.82 per TA household in Newham vs £8,604.26 in Wiltshire. (FR 1.12)

## 4. Newham vs the rest of London

Only London-wide figures quoted in these documents. No other borough's policy is in the knowledge base, so a policy-by-policy comparison is not possible yet.

- TA rate: Newham 48 vs London 17 per 1,000 households. (HS p13)
- Private renting: about 1 in 3 Newham residents vs 1 in 4 in London. (HS p11) FR 1.10 says 54% of residents live in private rented housing. The figures conflict, probably because of different dates or definitions. Do not use both.
- Evictions: 3.2 vs 1.7 per 1,000 households. (HS p11)
- LHA-affordable share: Outer East London under 2% to under 10% depending on size, vs 8% for London overall. (HS p7)
- London scale: 62,670 households in TA; 68% of England's homeless households in TA are in London. (HS p10, older figure.) London councils spending £90m a month on TA, up almost 40%. (FR 1.8)
- Placements (2021): 63% in Newham, 34% in another London borough, 3% outside London. (HS p13)
- National policy: £39bn Social and Affordable Homes Programme 2026-2036, at least 60% Social Rent, up to 30% (£11.7bn) via the GLA for London. Limited acquisitions of existing homes allowed. Right to Buy reforms, including new-build exemption for 35 years. (NAT, Steps 1 and 4)

## 5. What we infer (c)

- Exits are the bottleneck, not entries alone. With about 600 social lets a year and about 6,500 households in TA, even if every let went to a TA household it would take about 11 years to clear today's TA. Not every let goes to TA households, so the real figure is worse. (Arithmetic on HS and FR figures.)
- Newham's main exit route is now private renting, and the barriers to that route (LHA gap, benefit cap, shrinking supply) are mostly set nationally.
- The strategy runs 2021-2026, so it expires this year. Whether a successor exists is not in the knowledge base.
