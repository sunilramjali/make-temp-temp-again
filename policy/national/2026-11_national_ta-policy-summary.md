# National (England) temporary accommodation policy: summary and data needs

House London #3, 7 Nov 2026. Companion to [Newham TA policy summary](../newham/2026-11_newham_ta-policy-summary.md). Problem: how to make temporary accommodation (TA) temporary again, by moving households into settled homes.

Source key:
- NPEH = MHCLG, A National Plan to End Homelessness, CP 1452 (11 Dec 2025). Read via the gov.uk web version, not the PDF. Check exact wording against the PDF before quoting in the brief.
- NAT = MHCLG, Delivering a decade of renewal for social and affordable housing (July 2025). Uploaded copy.
- CoG = Homelessness Code of Guidance for Local Authorities (gov.uk, amended often; a 15 Oct 2026 PDF exists).
- AoA = Allocation of accommodation: guidance for local housing authorities in England (version dated 1 April 2026).
- HA96 = Housing Act 1996. HRA17 = Homelessness Reduction Act 2017. RRA25 = Renters' Rights Act 2025.
- SAO03 / SAO12 = Homelessness (Suitability of Accommodation) (England) Orders 2003 and 2012.
- CRF = Newham CRF Housing Payment Policy, April 2026 (local, used here only for national benefit rules it restates).

Labels: (a) what policy says should happen, (b) what the documents report happened, (c) our inference.

## 1. Headline national position (b)

- B&B and nightly-paid TA spend rose from £309m (2023/24) to £620m (2024/25), nearly half of councils' TA spend. (NPEH 1.2)
- Total homelessness spend £3.7bn in 2024/25, of which £2.8bn on TA. (NPEH 1.2)
- 2,070 families in B&B-type accommodation beyond the 6-week legal limit. (NPEH 1.1)
- Over 42,000 households placed in another area; 81% of them from London boroughs. (NPEH 1.1)
- Over 165,000 children in TA; over 1.3 million people on social housing registers. (NAT, intro)

## 2. The legal framework (a)

How a household gets into TA and out again:
- HA96 Part 7: if the council accepts the main homelessness duty, it must secure TA until the duty ends. Exits include a final offer of social housing or a private rented sector offer.
- HRA17: prevention (56 days) and relief duties before the main duty.
- HA96 s193(7AA) to (7F), added by the Localism Act 2011: councils can end the main duty with a private rented sector offer without the applicant's consent. The home must be suitable under SAO12 Article 3 (condition, safety, management, location).
- SAO03: B&B is not suitable for families or pregnant women except where nothing else is available, and then for no more than 6 weeks.
- HA96 Part 6 (allocations): homeless households are a "reasonable preference" category for social housing (s166A(3)). (AoA)

Check before quoting: SAO12 was written around fixed-term assured shorthold tenancies of at least 12 months. RRA25 abolished fixed terms from 1 May 2026. Shelter Legal describes a two-year re-application rule as applying to offers made before 1 May 2026. How private rented offers work now needs confirming against the current Code of Guidance.

## 3. National Plan to End Homelessness (Dec 2025) (a)

Targets (NPEH, Executive Summary):
- Increase the proportion of households supported to stay in their home or find alternative accommodation. The settled accommodation rate rose from 42.3% to 46.5%; no end figure is given in the text we read.
- Eliminate the unlawful use of B&Bs for families, apart from very short emergency stays, by the end of this parliament.
- Halve long-term rough sleeping by the end of this parliament.
- Progress reports at least every two years.

TA quality and supply:
- Local Authority Housing Fund round 4: £950m, aiming for up to 5,000 homes by 2030.
- Emergency Accommodation Reduction Programme: £30m.
- Apply the Decent Homes Standard to TA where possible; consider how Awaab's Law applies.
- Consider regulating the use of nightly-paid accommodation.
- TA Toolkit for sourcing and procurement; new metrics on suitability of TA placements.

Rules for councils on placements:
- Place households in their own area wherever possible; avoid multiple moves; never decide out-of-area placements on cost alone.
- New duty to notify schools, health visitors and GPs when a child is placed in TA.
- Family TA must have space for a cot for babies under two (£10.9m fund for 61 councils).
- Every council to publish an action plan with local targets aligned to a new Outcomes Framework, including TA procurement and allocation.

Moving people out of TA (the exit routes):
- Social supply: £39bn Social and Affordable Homes Programme, about 300,000 homes, about 180,000 for Social Rent. (NPEH 2.1; NAT Step 1)
- Allocations: for homes funded by that programme, providers expected to give councils 100% of first lets for nomination, unless agreed otherwise. Allocations guidance to be updated. (NPEH 2.2)
- Government will consider levers, including legislation, to require social landlords to rehouse statutory homeless households referred by councils. (NPEH 2.2)
- Review use of empty and under-occupied social homes. (NPEH 2.2; NAT Step 3)
- Private renting: RRA25 abolishes Section 21; LHA rates "kept under review". No commitment to raise LHA in the text we read. (NPEH 2.4, 2.5)
- Supported housing: £124m over 2026/27 to 2028/29. (NPEH 2.3)
- No specific national move-on target or length-of-stay target appears in the text we read.

Funding:
- £3.5bn for homelessness services, 2026/27 to 2028/29, mostly through the multi-year local government settlement.
- £969m for TA moved from the Homelessness Prevention Grant into the Revenue Support Grant over three years, using a formula reflecting TA need.
- New Homelessness, Rough Sleeping and Domestic Abuse Grant: almost £2bn over three years, ringfenced for prevention, meant to end the trade-off between prevention and TA costs.
- Crisis and Resilience Fund from 1 April 2026; housing element kept at £100m a year; Discretionary Housing Payments integrated in phases. (NPEH 2.5) Newham's CRF Policy confirms this replaced DHP locally.
- Housing Benefit subsidy for TA: only a commitment to consider a more sustainable funding model; no specific reform.

## 4. Social housing supply (a)

From NAT (July 2025):
- Step 1: £39bn SAHP 2026-2036; "at least 60% of homes delivered will be for Social Rent"; up to 30% of funding (up to £11.7bn) via the GLA for London. Limited acquisitions of existing homes allowed.
- Step 2: social rents may rise by CPI+1% a year from April 2026, for 10 years.
- Step 4: Right to Buy reforms: eligibility from 3 to 10 years' tenancy; new-build social homes exempt for 35 years; councils can combine RtB receipts with grant from 2026-27.
- Step 3: review allocations, void times and mutual exchanges.

## 5. Private renting and benefits (a)

- RRA25: Royal Assent 27 Oct 2025; first phase (tenancy reform, including ending Section 21) in force from 1 May 2026; later phases include a landlord database and ombudsman.
- Benefit cap in London: £23,000 a year (restated in Newham CRF Policy 2.7).
- LHA set by Broad Rental Market Area, not borough (CRF 2.7). Newham sits in Outer East London.

## 6. Roadblocks the national documents acknowledge (b)

- Councils forced to choose between prevention and TA costs (NPEH, funding section).
- Rising reliance on B&B and nightly-paid TA; spend doubled in one year (NPEH 1.2).
- Out-of-area placements concentrated in London (NPEH 1.1).
- Weakened capacity of councils and housing associations to build; fewer than 40% of council homes sold under Right to Buy since 2012 replaced (NAT Steps 2 and 4).

## 7. Where national policy meets Newham (c, our inference)

- The national plan targets entry to TA (prevention) and the worst TA (B&B), but sets no target for length of stay or move-on. That is exactly Newham's problem: most households wait "several years" (Newham web pages).
- The two national exit routes are social lets and private rented offers. In Newham, social lets are about 600 a year; private renting is limited by LHA and the benefit cap, which are set nationally and only "kept under review".
- The new supply money is long term (to 2036). The £950m LAHF round is the nearest-term national tool for TA supply.
- The 100% first-lets nomination rule only applies to newly funded homes, so it will not change the flow of existing relets.
- Health lens: the new duty to notify GPs and health visitors when a child enters TA creates a possible health data link, but we do not know if it is collected or published.
